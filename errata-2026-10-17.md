# B. Disclosures (cannot be fixed without new runs)

## B1. Improved denoising diffusion trains its reverse variance 1000x too weakly

`improved_ddpm.training_loss` samples one timestep and returns
`l_simple + lam * l_vlb` with `lam = 1e-3`, with no rescaling by $T$. The reference
implementation applies coefficient $1$ to the single sampled term, which is what
$\lambda = 0.001$ on the full $T$-term sum reduces to at $T=1000$. The learned reverse
variance is therefore close to untrained. The term is also computed with denoised clipping
enabled, where the reference disables it.

Suggested footnote: *Our improved-denoising implementation weights the sampled
variational term at $10^{-3}$ without rescaling by the number of timesteps, where the
reference weighting is equivalent to unity at $T=1000$; its learned reverse variance is
correspondingly undertrained. The baseline nonetheless reaches $6.04$ FID at fifty
evaluations against the bridge's $9.83$, so the deviation does not flatter the comparison.*

## B2. Variance restoration does not fire on the final step

`bridge.py` forces `eta = 0` on the last step of the exact-posterior sampler regardless of
the supplied variance profile, so the endpoint-uncertainty correction is applied on every
step but the last. The reported gains are gains of that variant. Either say so, or apply
the full correction and re-sample — re-sampling only, no retraining.

## B3. DDIM after clipping

Both DDPM samplers retain the network's raw epsilon after clipping the reconstructed
$x_0$, where the reference rederives epsilon from the clipped $x_0$. This bears on the
*explanation* offered for DDIM degradation at high step counts, not on any measured
number. If the explanation is load-bearing, soften it to an observation.
